Ethics & safety charter

          
Design and use principles. This research preview is not authorized for clinical use; regulatory classification and independent review are pending.

        

        
      


      
Principles the software follows today

      
    
        
  • First, do no harm. ClinLoop never diagnoses, orders or treats. It shows which follow-up a result requires and whether it happened; a clinician decides and acts.
    • 
        
  • Rules, not black boxes. Every alert comes from a written rule with its guideline reference and deadline. The same record always gives the same answer.
    • 
        
  • Specialist sign-off. A rule is marked “unsigned” until a specialist approves its current version; any change to the rule needs a new sign-off.
    • 
        
  • Says when it does not know. When a record is too sparse to judge, the loop is marked needs human review instead of guessed.
    • 
        
  • People stay in charge of patients. A message to a patient never names a diagnosis or result, and is sent only after a clinician approves it.
    • 
        
  • Everything is recorded. Each action (acknowledge, defer with a coded reason, close with evidence) goes into a tamper-evident audit trail.
    • 
        
  • Patient data stays in the hospital. No patient data is sent to an external AI service. Literature searches send rule-level search terms only.
    • 
        
  • Fair to every patient. The missed-follow-up rate is shown by language, age group and sex, so a group being missed becomes visible.
    • 
      


      
What has not been shown yet

      
    
        
  • No clinical validation on real hospital data, and no outcome evidence (no effect on patient outcomes, safety events, cost or workload has been measured).
    • 
        
  • No regulatory review (MFDS / FDA). Not for use in patient care.
    • 
        
  • Accuracy so far comes from synthetic and independently written test cases (docs/ACCURACY_EVALUATION.md).
    • 
      


      
Before any clinical use

      
    
        
  1. Ethics committee (IRB) approval and a registered study protocol (docs/STUDY_PROTOCOL.md).
    2. 
        
  2. Specialist sign-off of every rule used, at the partner hospital.
    3. 
        
  3. Silent (shadow) running against chart review, before any clinician sees an alert.
    4. 
        
  4. Independent review of results, and the applicable regulatory pathway.
    5.
