# Does ClinLoop work on a whole hospital record? A test on EHR-style data

**Question:** fed a complete electronic health record (every visit, lab result, prescription, diagnosis,
procedure and note over a lifetime), does ClinLoop:
- run without errors;
- find the follow-up obligations a clinician would find;
- give each one the right status;
- keep the worklist free of noise?

**Short answer:**
- **Robustness:** no crashes on 144,758 records of 120 patients, or on the 713 official HL7 FHIR example records
  (124 resource types).
- **First pass:** the app agreed poorly with an independent reviewer. It found 87 of the reviewer's 210
  obligations and raised 1,048 loops, mostly noise. Prescription renewals were read as new drugs, so known
  heart failure looked new, and kidney-dialysis creatinine looked like acute kidney injury.
- **After fixing what the comparison showed:**
  - it finds 149 of 210 (71%);
  - 123 of those have the same status as the reviewer's;
  - it raises 194 loops in total, and 42 of the 45 extra ones trace back to a known defect in this data set.
  - Through the real hospital import path, the worklist for 120 patients holds 14 current items.
- **Safety:** no obligation was shown as done when it was not. One disagreement is discussed below.

## What was tested

**Data:** we had no access to a hospital's database. The closest available stand-in was used:
- **Main data set:** the SMART on FHIR sample bulk export, 100 patients (120 including family members in the
  export), generated by Synthea. Synthea simulates lifetime records, including:
  - encounters (outpatient, emergency, inpatient, ICU);
  - 70,233 observations;
  - 7,263 prescriptions;
  - procedures, conditions and 7,761 clinical notes;
  - deaths.

  It is the format a hospital exports through the FHIR bulk-data API.
- **Robustness check:** the 713 clinical example resources of the HL7 FHIR R4 specification.
- **Not reachable from the test environment:** real de-identified hospital data (MIMIC-IV on FHIR, PhysioNet).
  That is the next test to run.

**Method:**
1. Each patient's whole record goes through the app's own pipeline:
   - FHIR → events → the 58 rules → loops;
   - and the hospital import path (`POST /api/v1/fhir/ingest`).
2. **An independent reviewer**, acting as an internist and clinical informatician, worked blind:
   - they received only the rule definitions and the raw records, never the code;
   - they wrote their own analysis;
   - they listed every obligation with its status at the export date, recording each clinical judgement.
3. The two lists were compared obligation by obligation (patient, rule, triggering record). Each disagreement
   was traced to its cause. App defects were fixed and each fix has a test (`tests/test_hospital_data.py`).

To reproduce (after downloading the export):
```
python tests/hospital_eval/run_bulk.py <export dir> out.json
python tests/hospital_eval/compare.py out.json
```

## Results

| | First pass | After fixes |
|---|---|---|
| Reviewer's obligations found by the app | 87 / 210 | **149 / 210 (71%)** |
| … with the same status (done / done late / not done) | 73 | **123** |
| Loops the app raised | 1,048 | **194** |
| Shown as done when not done (false closures) | 1 | 1, which on review was a real visit (see below) |
| Current worklist through the hospital path (120 patients) | n/a | **14 items** |

### Defects the comparison found, all fixed

**Noise:**
- **Prescription renewals were read as new drugs.**
  - EHRs write a new prescription for every refill. For 120 patients, this opened 761 "check labs after a new
    drug" loops.
  - Now only a new drug, a new dose, a new route or a restart counts. A dose reduction does not.
- **Heart failure already known** was treated as a new diagnosis at every admission.
- **Creatinine on dialysis** raised acute-kidney-injury warnings, and dialysis-period values formed the
  baseline. Now both are excluded, and a kidney transplant is watched again.
- **An ED visit that became an admission, and overlapping encounters for one stay,** counted as two discharges.
- **Decades-old history:** a 1977 PSA reached today's worklist. Hospital paths now leave out obligations whose
  deadline passed more than `CLINLOOP_HISTORY_DAYS` (default 365) ago.
- **Patients who died:** obligations that fell due after death lapsed; they were not missed. A
  death-certification encounter is not a clinic visit.

**Misses:**
- **A referral never opened its "visit within 30 days" loop (R008).** A rule that fires on any event of a type
  needed the event to carry a condition.
- **Monitoring after a drug start (R010) covered five named drugs.** It now covers drug classes: ACE inhibitors,
  ARBs, MRAs, diuretics, calcineurin inhibitors and chemotherapy. Any laboratory's specimen code is matched (for
  example "creatinine in blood"). The lab is due when the drug's guidance says: TSH 8 weeks after levothyroxine,
  a lithium level after 1 week. Metformin was removed, because renal function is checked before it, not after.
- **A completed prostate biopsy did not close the PSA rule (R004),** while any referral, even to cardiology,
  did. Now only urology or oncology, or a prostate biopsy, closes it.
- **Pre-eclampsia diagnosed before delivery** did not trigger the postpartum blood-pressure check (R024),
  because the diagnosis was already marked resolved at delivery.
- **Referrals recorded as procedures** ("Referral to cardiology service") were ignored.

**Silence:**
- **Lab results with no abnormal flag and no reference range** cannot raise abnormal-result alerts, and the app
  said nothing about it.
  - Now a range is used when there is no flag.
  - A feed with neither is reported: *"N numeric lab results carry no interpretation flag and no reference
    range"*.
  - Every lab result in this data set is like that. So abnormal-lab rules (R001, R002, R021, R022) could not
    be tested here; they are tested on the synthetic and report sets.

### Remaining disagreements

**Referral visits (R008), 25 cases:**
- **What happened:** this data never records which specialty a clinic visit was in. The reviewer accepted the
  next visit for the referred problem. The app does not assume a specialist visit took place, so it shows these
  referrals as still open.
- **Why the app does this:** guessing could hide a missed referral, while a false alarm costs one look.
- **In a hospital:** visits carry a department, and this difference disappears.

**Lisinopril 10 mg and 20 mg prescribed side by side, 25 cases:**
- **What happened:** the reviewer counted each 20 mg prescription as a dose increase. The app counts the first
  one and treats the rest as renewals.
- **What it is:** a Synthea prescribing pattern.

**Oral ↔ intravenous switches during admissions, 19 cases:**
- **What happened:** the reviewer counted every switch back to the usual oral drug. The app counts a return to a
  product taken in the past year as a renewal.
- **What it is:** a judgement call.

**Creatinine series (R053), 53 cases:**
- **What happened:** this data set has two inconsistent creatinine series under one test code, for example 0.6
  and 3.2 mg/dL a day apart, with an eGFR of 80. The reviewer separated them by panel; the app compares results
  of the same test code. 42 of the app's extra alerts are this artefact.
- **In a hospital:** one test code means one assay.

**The one "done" the reviewer disagreed with:**
- **What happened:** after a heart-failure stay, the app found an outpatient check-up 4 days after discharge
  and closed the 7-day-visit obligation. The reviewer did not count a routine check-up.
- **Judgement:** the visit happened within the window, so the app's reading stands. But a hospital may want
  only cardiology or heart-failure clinic visits to count.

## Data-quality problems the reviewer found in this data set

These would also confuse a real system and are worth checking in any hospital feed:
- creatinine values above 20 mg/dL;
- HbA1c below 4% in half the results;
- troponin in the wrong unit;
- ED visits and admissions duplicated;
- clinical notes stored twice;
- 7,760 COVID-19 test orders with no results, most dated before 2020.

## Limits

- **Synthetic data.** Synthea's records are realistic in structure but not in clinical detail. Its labs carry no
  flags, its visits no specialty, and its notes contain no follow-up plans. Its radiology has no report text.
- **What remains untested here.** Report reading, note reading and abnormal-lab rules were not tested on this
  data. They are covered by the blind report and timeline sets ([`ACCURACY_EVALUATION.md`](ACCURACY_EVALUATION.md)).
- **One reviewer**, who is a model acting as a clinician. A hospital study needs two clinicians on the hospital's
  own records.
- **Next step:** the same comparison on real de-identified hospital data. For example:
  - MIMIC-IV on FHIR (PhysioNet, credentialed access);
  - or a hospital's own export in shadow mode, against chart review by two specialists.
